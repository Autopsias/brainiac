Why the revision loop is paused

A revision 6 aimed at recall would be written against a corpus whose speaker labels are wrong in all eight meetings. Some of the 181 estimated misses belong to the doctrine and some belong to the transcript, and the rows alone cannot separate them. Loosening the rules now would fit a transcription artefact rather than predict anything — the same trap that produced a measured miss factor of 3.6 in an earlier round.

A separate defect sits behind it: the transcription pipeline's speaker validation cannot detect a collapse, so it has never reported one. That is a pipeline defect, not a doctrine defect.

One suspicion was checked rather than assumed. 20 of 51 duplicate drops repeat verbatim in the transcript, but 18 of those sit in one meeting where an automatic-transcription loop repeats a 90-second block seven times. Outside it the figure is 2 of 30, so the new deduplication rule is sound.

Revision 5 is much more conservative, and the cost is unmeasured. It staged 10 rows from 266; revision 4 staged 39 from 265 on the other corpus. The limit

A promise neither reader emitted cannot appear in any bucket, so it is unmeasurable here. MISSED is measured over what pass B or the agreement sample surfaced and is therefore a lower bound. The agreement sample is the only probe that could have touched that joint blind spot; 0 of its 11 rows reached the owner, so the joint blind spot is not measured at all by this run.

The two readers are the same model family, so their errors are correlated and two readings are worth materially less than two independent ones. This plan did not quantify that correlation and asserts no figure for it — direction only: it pushes the true miss rate above what is reported. The Wilson intervals ignore stratification and meeting clustering, so they are a floor on the uncertainty, not a full account of it.

Open question closed: pass B should not have run on a different vendor's model — doing so would have exported restricted client transcripts outside this project's data boundary, which the plan forbids outright. That holds regardless of how much independence a second vendor would have bought, a quantity this plan never measured.

Corpus ceiling — a finding in its own right

45 transcript files, 34,479 turns total; at ~1 staged candidate per 1,400 turns that projects ~25 staged candidates even if every file were readable. Only 10 files pass the usability rule (6,618 turns, ~4.7 projected); this draw took 9 of them (6,230 turns, ~4.5 projected; 6 actually staged). Relaxing to “owner named at least once” adds 11 duplicate-owner meetings (9,831 turns) and reaches only ~12. s01's power table needs ~20 for a HIGH result and ~40 for a LOW one. No sample this corpus can supply resolves the 0.2 INVENTED rule, at any N — more meetings is not a route to a decision. MISSED is not bound by that ceiling: its denominator comes from pass B's 349-row list, not from pass A's staged rows.
